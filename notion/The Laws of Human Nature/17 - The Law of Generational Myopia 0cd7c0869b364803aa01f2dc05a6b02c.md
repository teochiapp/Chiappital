# 17 - The Law of Generational Myopia

Propietario: Teo Chiappero

### Seize the Historical Moment

We shall call this knowledge generational awareness. To attain it, first we must understand the actual profound effect that our generation has on how we view the world, and second we must understand the larger generational patterns that shape history and recognize where our time period fits into the overall scheme.

When it comes to the changes generated by the tensions between two generations, we can say that the greater part of them will come from the young.

A child and an adult experience time very differently—for the former it moves rather slowly, and all too quickly for the latter.

In our youth we experience life more intensely. Most of us tend to be outwardly focused, concerned with what people might think of us and with how we fit in.
As we get older, the intensity diminishes, our minds tend to tighten up around certain conventional ideas and beliefs

Some distance from life, some self-control, and perhaps the wisdom that comes from accumulating experiences.

As we age, we can strive to retain the positive youthful qualities that often fade with the years. For instance, we can regain some of the natural curiosity we had as children by dropping some of the smugness and know-it-all attitude that often come over us as we get older.

Present generations: Your goal here is to be less a product of the times and to gain the ability to transform your relationship to your generation. 

If you are younger, you try to interact more with those of older generations.
If you are older, you reverse this by actively interacting with those of a younger generation, not as a parent or authority figure but as a peer.

> A man’s shortcomings are taken from his epoch; his virtues and greatness belong to himself.
        – Johann Wolfgang von Goethe
