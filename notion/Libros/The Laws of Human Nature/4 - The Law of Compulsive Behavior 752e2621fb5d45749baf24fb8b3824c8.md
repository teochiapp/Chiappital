### Determine the Strength of People’s Character

People’s character is formed in their earliest years and by their daily habits. It is what compels them to repeat certain actions in their lives and fall into negative patterns. Look closely at such patterns and remember that people never do something just once. They will inevitably repeat their behavior.

It is not spirits or gods that control us but rather our character.

The ability to observe people’s character—as seen in their actions and patterns—is an absolutely critical social skill.

But it is a skill you must consciously develop, because we humans are generally inept when it comes to such assessments.

You must always keep in mind the primary corollary of this law: people never do something just once.

 If they are late in finishing simple assignments, they will be late with larger projects. If they become irritated by little inconveniences, they will tend to crumble under larger ones

In everyday life people can often do well at disguising their character flaws, but in times of stress or crisis these flaws can suddenly become very apparent.

 “If you want  to test a man’s character, give him power.”

 So often we think that power has changed people, when in fact it simply reveals more of who they are.

Extroverts are largely governed by external criteria. The question that dominates them is “What do others think of me?”

Extroverts value external things—good clothes, great meals, concrete enjoyment shared with others.

Introverts are more sensitive and easily exhausted by too much outward activity.

Introverts and extroverts do not naturally understand each other.

 Keep in mind that there are generally more extroverts in the world.
