# The Almanack of Naval Ravickant

Propietario: Teo Chiappero
Etiquetas: Finanzas, Productividad
Autor: Eric Jorgenson

## Building Wealth

Making money is not a thing you do—it’s a skill you learn.

Wealth is having assets that earn while you sleep

You can only achieve mastery in one or two things. It’s usually things you’re obsessed about.

If they can train you to do it, then eventually they will train a computer to do it.

You know how to do it better because you love it, and no one can compete with you.

## Building Judgement

My definition of wisdom is knowing the long-term consequences of your actions.

I think the smartest people can explain things to a child.

To see the truth, you have to get your ego out of the way because your ego doesn’t want to face the truth.

During decision-making, the brain is a memory prediction machine.

What you want is principles. You want mental models.

If you can’t decide, the answer is no.

So you generally want to lean into things with short-term pain, but long-term gain.

I don’t want to read everything. I just want to read the 100 great books over and over again.

“As long as I have a book in my hand, I don’t feel like I’m wasting time.”

To think clearly, understand the basics.

## Learning Happiness

Don’t take yourself so seriously. You’re just a monkey with a plan.

A rational person can find peace by cultivating indifference to things outside of their control.

The reality is life is a single-player game. You’re born alone. You’re going to die alone. All of your interpretations are alone. All your memories are alone. You’re gone in three generations, and nobody cares.

In any situation in life, you always have three choices: you can change it, you can accept it, or you can leave it.

How do you learn to accept things you can’t change?
Fundamentally, it boils down to one big hack: embracing death.

“I don’t have time” is just another way of saying “It’s not a priority.”

Life-hack: When in bed, meditate. Either you will have a deep meditation or fall asleep. Victory either way.

Journaling is writing meditation.

Impatience with actions, patience with results.

Set up systems, not goals.

Inspiration is perishable—act on it immediately.

The best book is the one you’ll devour.
