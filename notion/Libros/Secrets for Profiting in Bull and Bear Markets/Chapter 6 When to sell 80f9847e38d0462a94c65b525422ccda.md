# Chapter 6: When to sell

Propietario: Teo Chiappero

To complete the journey, there is one more very important step to master—learning when to sell.

The problem is that an investor or trader often sells for no other reason than his feeling that the stock is too high.

DON'TS FOR SELLING

1. Don't base your selling decision on tax considerations.

2. Don't base your selling decision on how much the stock is yielding.  The selloff wipes out the whole year's worth of dividends plus more.

3. Don't hold onto a stock because the price!earnings (PIE) ratio is low.

4. Don't sell a stock simply because the PIE is too high. They always want rational answers why a stock moves. 

Investors are like manic-depressives: when they become optimistic, they'll find excuses to buy stocks no matter how high the P/E gets. So the secret is to accept that too high can become

much higher when a stock is in Stage 2, and hold back on your selling until you see the whites of their eyes and Stage 3 starts to form.

5. Don't average down in a negative situation. 

Learning how to handle a losing situation and keeping losses to a minimum is one of the most important secrets of successful investing.

Every time you average in the hope of hitting the bottom, you simply add more shares and more losses to your portfolio.

Trying to guess the bottom in a weak stock is like fishing in the ocean with your fingers

If you follow this winning discipline, you'll still have the capital to make up your loss on future purchases.

6. Don't refuse to sell because the overall market trend is bullish.

" Don't confuse brains with a bull market."

7. Don't wait for the next rally to sell. Don't wait around trying to recoup an extra point or two on a rally. 

8. Don't hold onto a stock simply because it is of high quality. 

The protective sell-stop is an incredibly helpful and profitable technique when properly used.

Sometimes it will be 8 percent below your purchase price, other times 12 percent or more. The percentage will not be the determining factor. Instead, the prior support level and 30-week MA will be your two keys to focus on.

But here's a little secret that I've learned over the years which has served me well. If the sell-stop should be placed right above a round number, or on it, set it instead just under the round number.

Many investors like to buy at round numbers. 

From now on, never hold a stock without a protective sellstop.

LEARNING HOW TO WIN BY LEARNING HOW TO LOSE

Specialists and other pros don't lose sleep if a given position ends up in the loss column.

They concern themselves with two far more important factors: (1) the net result of all their positions (as long as it is strongly positive they are happy), and (2) where they will call it quits if a given stock moves against them.

What drives us to this destructive behavior? In one word: ego! 
For ego-driven investors, even means "I wasn't d u m b . " 

The lesson is obvious. Protect all of your positions with stops and don't ever make an exception because a certain stock is a special situation.
