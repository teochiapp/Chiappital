### 2: MONEY AIN’T WHAT IT USED TO BE—AND NEVER WAS

Money is something we choose to trade our life energy for.

Our life energy is our allotment of time here on Earth, the hours of precious life available to us. When we go to our jobs we are trading our life energy for money.

Men do not desire to be rich, only to be richer than other men. In other words, as soon as rich becomes available to the likes of us, it will no longer be rich.

We have established that money is simply something you trade life energy for. Now let’s look at how much life energy (in hours) you are currently trading for how much money (in dollars)—i.e., how much money are you making for the amount of time you work?

The methodology for this marvel of monetary metaphysics is: Keep track of every cent that comes into or goes out of your life.

“Why,” you may ask, “do I need to go to such great lengths?” Because it’s the best way to become conscious of how money actually comes and goes in your life as opposed to how you think it comes and goes.

### 7: FOR LOVE OR MONEY: VALUING YOUR LIFE ENERGY—WORK AND INCOME

Work is defined as something that people do not want to do and money as the reward that compensates for the unpleasantness of work.

For most of human history, people only worked for two or three hours per day. As we moved from agriculture to industrialization, work hours increased, creating standards that label a person lazy if he or she doesn’t work a forty-hour week.... The very notion that everyone should have a job only began with the Industrial Revolution .6

Breaking the link between work and wages has as much power in our lives as the recognition that money is simply “something we trade our life energy for.” Money is our life energy;

“My job is not the main event of my life.”
Breaking the link between work and money gives you back your life

Now that we’ve established that the only intrinsic purpose of paid employment is getting paid (whether you love or hate your job), it makes sense to see whether you are trading your precious life energy for what it’s worth.

You want more money so that you can have enough material possessions and more life

Step 7 is simply “valuing your life energy and increasing your income”—since the only purpose of paid employment is getting paid. You do this not out of greed or competition but out of self-respect and an appreciation of life. As a by-product you might well find yourself with less debt, more savings, more free time, more energy on the job, more energy off the job, more satisfied clients, a more satisfied family and more peace of mind.
