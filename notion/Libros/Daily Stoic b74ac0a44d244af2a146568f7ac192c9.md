## Introduction

The Discipline of Perception (how we see and perceive the world around us)
The Discipline of Action (the decisions and actions we take—and to what end)
The Discipline of Will (how we deal with the things we cannot change, attain clear and convincing judgment, and come to a true understanding of our place in the world)

JANUARY: CLARITY

WATCHING THE WISE

That is the role of wise people in our lives—to serve as model and inspiration.

Pick someone, watch what they do (and what they don’t do), and do your best to do the same.

KEEP IT SIMPLE: let’s focus just on what’s in front of us.

YOU DON’T HAVE TO STAY ON TOP OF EVERYTHING: How much more rested and present would you feel if you were no longer excited and outraged by every scandal, breaking story, and potential crisis

Stoicism is designed to be medicine for the soul

MARCH: AWARENESS

> “Yesterday’s the past, tomorrow’s the future, but today is a gift. That’s why it’s called the present.”
> 

“Wherever you go, there you are.”

We can turn on some music and tune out the world. We can turn off technology or shut off those rampant thoughts in our head. That will provide us peace. Nothing else.

WEALTH AND FREEDOM ARE FREE: “. . . freedom isn’t secured by filling up on your heart’s desire but by removing your desire.”

There are two ways to be wealthy—to get everything you want or to want everything you have.

If you chafe and fight and struggle for more, you will never be free.

> “We buy things we don’t need, to impress people we don’t like.”
> 

APRIL: UNBIASED THOUGHT

If you hold a perpetually negative outlook, soon enough everything you encounter will seem negative. Close it off and you’ll become closed-minded. Color it with the wrong thoughts and your life will be dyed the same.

Uninvited guests might arrive at your home, but you don’t have to ask them to stay for dinner. You don’t have to let them into your mind.

We’re not as smart and as wise as we’d like to think we are. If we ever do want to become wise, it comes from the questioning and from humility

“Throw out your conceited opinions, for it is impossible for a person to begin to learn what he thinks he already knows.”

But what if we let these opinions go? Let’s try weeding them out of our lives so that things simply are. Not good or bad, not colored with opinion or judgment. Just are.

Be willing to learn from anyone and everyone, regardless of their station in life.

REAL GOOD IS SIMPLE: wisdom, self-control, justice, courage

Attention is a habit, and that letting your attention slip and wander builds bad habits and enables mistakes.
You’ll never complete all your tasks if you allow yourself to be distracted with every tiny interruption. Your attention is one of your most critical resources.

To be rational today, we have to do just three things:
First, we must look inward.
Next, we must examine ourselves critically.
Finally, we must make our own decisions—uninhibited by biases or popular notions.

> “If anyone can prove and show to me that I think and act in error, I will gladly change it—for I seek the truth, by which no one has ever been harmed. The one who is harmed is the one who abides in deceit and ignorance.”
> 

No one should be ashamed at changing his mind—that’s what the mind is for.

Character is a powerful defense in a world that would love to be able to seduce you, buy you, tempt you, and change you.

MAY: RIGHT ACTION

A Stoic has no uniform and resembles no stereotype. They are not identifiable by look or by sight or by sound.
The only way to recognize them? By their character.

When someone asks you what you did yesterday, do you really want the answer to be “nothing”?

We should take pleasure from our actions—in taking the right actions—rather than the results that come from them.

If you read the few great books deeply instead of briefly skimming all the new books? Your shelves might be emptier, but your brain and your life would be fuller.

Evaluate what you are doing, why you are doing it, and where accomplishing it will take you

JUNE: PROBLEM SOLVING

“if anything can go wrong, it will.”

two kinds of people:

- Why them? Why not me?
- If they can do it, why can’t I?

See the success of others as an inspiration

LISTENING ACCOMPLISHES MORE THAN SPEAKING: ‘The reason why we have two ears and only one mouth is so we might listen more and talk less.’”

That’s who you want to be, whatever your line of work: the casual, relaxed person in every situation who tells everyone else to take a breath and not to worry.

TAKE A WALK: Nourish yourself and your mind and solve your problems along the way.

Is there any reason to fight about this? Is arguing going to help solve anything?

Just think of what you could accomplish—and how much better you would feel—if you could conquer the need to fight and win every tiny little thing.

If someone hurts you, it’s a chance to practice forgiveness.
If something is hard, it is a chance to get stronger.
Try this line of thinking and see whether there is a situation in which one could not find some virtue to practice or derive some benefit. There isn’t one. Every impediment can advance action in some form or another.

JULY: DUTY

Kidding aside, it might not seem like it makes a big difference to see life as something you have to do versus get to do, but there is.

“In your actions, don’t procrastinate. In your conversations, don’t confuse. In your thoughts, don’t wander. In your soul, don’t be passive or aggressive

Simple is rarely easy

FORGIVE THEM BECAUSE THEY DON’T KNOW: Use this knowledge to be gentle and gracious.

We are made for cooperation (synergia) with each other.

“Receive without pride, let go without attachment.”

If there is something you can actually do to help these suffering people, then, yes, the disturbing news (and your reaction to it) has relevance to your reasoned choice

> “Work is what horses die of. Everybody should know that.”
> 

You just had a head start over some people. That’s why it is our duty to understand and be patient with others.

Wouldn’t you like to be great of soul, filled with confidence, and invincible to external events? Then practice your philosophy.

That’s Stoic joy—the joy that comes from purpose, excellence, and duty.

AUGUST : PRAGMATISM

“The more you say,” Robert Greene has written, “the more likely you are to say something foolish.”

We’re never going to be perfect. Our pursuits should be aimed at progress

Your brain was designed to do this work. It was meant to separate what is important from what is senseless, to keep things in perspective, to only become troubled by that which is worth becoming troubled about.

The key to accomplishing that is to ruthlessly expunge the inessential from our live

Let the news come when it does. Be too busy working to care.

Don’t spend your time (the most valuable and least renewable of all your resources) on the things that don’t matter. What about the things that don’t matter but you’re absolutely obligated to do? Well, spend as little time and worry on them as possible.

Even when things were really bad, when the world made them want to weep in despair or rage, they chose to laugh about it.
Like Democritus, we can make that same choice. There is more humor than hate to be found in just about every situation. And at least humor is productive—making things less heavy, not more so.

> “No person has the power to have everything they want, but it is in their power not to want what they don’t have, and to cheerfully put to good use what they do have.”
> 

SEPTEMBER: FORTITUDE AND RESILIENCE

“let come what may”

“We must undergo a hard winter training and not rush into things for which we haven’t prepared.”

There are plenty of misfortunes one can practice, plenty of problems one can solve in advance.

Instead of asking for ‘a way to sleep with her,’ try asking for ‘a way to stop desiring to sleep with her.

NO PAIN, NO GAIN “Difficulties show a person’s character”

“I fear not the man who has practiced ten thousand kicks once,” he said, “but I fear the man who has practiced one kick ten thousand times.

Think about which behaviors you’d like to be able to default to if you could.

We’re all addicts in one way or another. We’re addicted to our routines, to our coffee, to our comfort, to someone else’s approval.

> “Anyone who truly wants to be free,” Epictetus said, “won’t desire something that is actually in someone else’s control, unless they want to be a slave.”
> 

Can you try going without this or that for a day?

Money doesn’t change people, it just makes them more of who they are. Robert Caro has written that “power doesn’t corrupt, it reveals.

OCTOBER: VIRTUE AND KINDNESS

Invests in themselves—in being a good and wise person.

You can always get up after you fall, but remember, what has been said can never be unsaid.

What lasts longer (and remains more within our circle of control)? Wisdom, good character, sobriety, and kindness.

> “The best way to avenge yourself is to not be like that.”
> 

A virtuous person is generous with assumptions: that something was an accident, that someone didn’t know, that it won’t happen again. This makes life easier to bear and makes us more tolerant.

As always, we’re going to focus on what we control: in this case, the ability to choose to respond with kindness.

Good habits have the power to drive out bad habits

Focus every bit of yourself on being the best person you can be in the present moment.

NOVEMBER: ACCEPTANCE / AMOR FATI

Amor fati (a love of fate). It’s not just accepting, it’s loving everything that happens.

Not only do even the most fortunate of us complain, it often seems like the more fortunate we are, the more time we have to do so.

To resent change is to wrongly assume that you have a choice in the matter.
Everything is change

FOUR HABITS OF THE STOIC MIND

1. Accept only what is true.

2.	Work for the common good
3.	Match our needs and wants with what is in our control.
4.	Embrace what nature has in store for us.

Pretend that each event—whether desired or unexpected—was willed to happen, willed specifically for you

> “There is one thing and only one thing that causes unhappiness. The name of that thing is Attachment.”
> 

If we’re Stoic, there is one thing we can be sure of: whatever happens, we’re going to be OK.

DECEMBER: MEDITATION ON MORTALITY

> ” Every person is born with a death sentence. Each second that passes by is one you’ll never get back.
> 

DON’T HIDE FROM YOUR FEELINGS: “It’s better to conquer grief than to deceive it.”

Use today. Use every day. Make yourself satisfied with what you have been given.

We are unfinished products up until the end

In death, no one is better, no one is worse. All our stories have the same finale.

Your life is plenty long—just use it properly.
